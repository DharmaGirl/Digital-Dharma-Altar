# 🪔 Digital Dharma Altar

A free, offline-first digital Buddhist altar you can use anywhere — as a webpage, or as a printed poster.

Made for practitioners who are traveling, need to keep their practice private, are just beginning to collect thangkas and sacred images, or simply want a portable altar they can carry everywhere.

---

## What This Is

A single HTML file. No installation. No account. No internet required after the first load. Everything stays on your device.

Open it in any browser and it becomes a functioning altar: add your thangka images to the frames, light the butter lamps, offer incense, and have seven water bowls ready. Or set up your images, take a screenshot, and print it as a poster to use wherever you are.

The structure follows the guidelines in FPMT's *[Preliminary Practice of Altar Set-Up and Water Bowl Offerings](https://atishacentre.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/altar_setup_and_water_bowls.pdf)*.

---

## How the Altar Is Arranged

### Top Shelf — Three large frames
For your **root guru, Guru Rinpoche (Padmasambhava), and other lineage teachers**.

### Middle Shelf — Three frames
Following the traditional three supports of enlightened body, speech, and mind:
- A **sacred text** (scripture — enlightened speech)
- **Buddha Śākyamuni** at center (enlightened body)
- A **stupa** to the right of Śākyamuni (enlightened mind)

### Bottom Shelf — Ten small frames
For your **yidams (meditation deities) and bodhisattvas** you work with — Tara, Chenrezig, Mañjuśrī, Vajrapāṇi, and so on.

## How to Use It

### As a webpage altar
1. Download `index.html`
2. Open it in any browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari — all work)
3. Click any frame to upload a thangka image from your device
4. Click the butter lamps individually to light them (they flicker 🕯)
5. Click the incense sticks or coil to offer incense (smoke rises)
6. The seven water bowls are already set

### Using the Dharma Player
Click the **☸ dharma player** button below the altar to open the audio panel.

- **+ mp3** next to each track slot — click to load an audio file from your device. The filename becomes the track name automatically.
- **Four track slots** — load up to four recitations, sutras, mantras, or chants
- **Full controls** — play, pause, previous, next, loop single track, volume
- **Progress bar** — click anywhere on it to seek
- **Auto-advance** — when a track ends it moves to the next loaded track

Everything plays locally from your device. No streaming, no internet required.

---

### As a printed poster
1. Set up your images in the frames
2. Take a screenshot (or use browser print → save as PDF)
3. Print it — A3 or A2 on matte paper looks especially good
4. Frame it or pin it wherever you practice

### Sharing with others
You can also host this on GitHub Pages for free, so anyone can open and use it directly in their browser without downloading anything. See below.

## Where to Find Free Dharma Audio

**FPMT (Foundation for the Preservation of the Mahayana Tradition)**
[fpmt.org/shop](https://fpmt.org/shop) — many recitations are free or pay-what-you-wish, including the Sutra of Golden Light, Heart Sutra, Vajrasattva mantra, and more. Search "audio" or "MP3" in their shop.

**Lama Yeshe Wisdom Archive**
[lamayeshe.com](https://www.lamayeshe.com) — free teachings and recitations from Lama Zopa Rinpoche and Lama Yeshe.

**84000: Translating the Words of the Buddha**
[84000.co](https://84000.co) — free translations of sutras. Some also have audio recitations.

**Garchen Buddhist Institute**
[garchen.net](https://garchen.net) — Garchen Rinpoche has made many teachings and recitations freely available.

**Lotsawa House**
[lotsawahouse.org](https://www.lotsawahouse.org) — extensive free library of Tibetan Buddhist texts and prayers in translation.

## Where to Find Free Thangka Images

Some teachers and centers offer high-quality sacred images freely for practice use:

- **Garchen Buddhist Institute** — [garchen.net](https://garchen.net) (Garchen Rinpoche has made many images available)
- **FPMT Image Resources** — [fpmt.org](https://fpmt.org)
- **Himalayan Art Resources** — [himalayanart.org](https://www.himalayanart.org) (scholarly archive, many images free for personal use)
- **Rigpa Shedra** — [rigpawiki.org](https://www.rigpawiki.org)

If you can't find a stupa image — a photograph of the Boudhanath Stupa or the Bodhgaya Mahābodhi stupa works beautifully, and represents the Buddha's enlightened mind on your altar.

## Hosting on GitHub Pages (free live URL)

1. Fork or clone this repository
2. Go to **Settings → Pages**
3. Set source to **main branch**
4. GitHub will generate a URL like `yourusername.github.io/digital-dharma-altar`

Anyone can then open it in their browser and use it without downloading anything.

## A Note on Intention

This altar was made for personal practice use and offered freely. The FPMT guide notes that the objects on an altar serve as a means for directing one's mind toward the Buddha's enlightened qualities. Whether the image is printed on paper, painted on silk, or displayed on a screen, what matters is the intention of the practitioner facing it.

If you are using a digital or printed version while traveling or keeping your practice private — that is enough. The merit field is present.
